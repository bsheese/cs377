# Supplemental activities

In-class activities that go with the course notebooks but are not part of a unit itself. Each notebook is self-contained and loads its data from this repo's raw GitHub URLs, like the unit notebooks.

| Notebook | Goes with | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| [Ridge_vs_Lasso_Pair](Ridge_vs_Lasso_Pair.ipynb) | [17_2_1_3 Regularization](../17_regression_crossval/17_2_1_MLR/17_2_1_3_MLR_Ames_Part3_Regularization.ipynb) | Pair activity on the Ames data: how Ridge and Lasso split the credit between near-identical "twin" features, why Lasso reaches exactly zero, and which penalty fits which situation. One partner writes short scikit-learn snippets in the notebook (hints included); the other records answers in the Canvas quiz. |
| [Nested_CV_Pair](Nested_CV_Pair.ipynb) | [17_2_1_4 Tuning](../17_regression_crossval/17_2_1_MLR/17_2_1_4_MLR_Ames_Part4_HyperparameterTuning.ipynb), [17_2_1_5 Nested CV](../17_regression_crossval/17_2_1_MLR/17_2_1_5_MLR_Ames_Part5_NestedCV.ipynb) | Pair activity on the Ames data: a recap of why Lasso reaches exactly zero, the two loops of nested cross-validation, why one test split can mislead (ten random splits against the nested estimate) and which model to deploy. One partner makes small edits to given scikit-learn code; the other records answers in the Canvas quiz. |
| [Nested_CV_Review](Nested_CV_Review.ipynb) | [Nested_CV_Pair](Nested_CV_Pair.ipynb) | Follow-up written after the Nested CV pair activity, for reading before Exam 2: works through the mistakes that came up in more than one pair (why Lasso stops at exactly zero, inner-loop sizes, pipeline grid keys, reading the fold alphas, why one test split is luck and not leakage, and what to deploy and report), with code that reproduces each number. Saved with outputs. |
| [Confusion_Matrix_Pair](Confusion_Matrix_Pair.ipynb) | [18_1_1 Foundations](../18_classification/18_1_Classification_Basics/18_1_1_Classification_Foundations.ipynb), [18_1_2 Confusion Matrix](../18_classification/18_1_Classification_Basics/18_1_2_Confusion_Matrix_Basic_Metrics.ipynb) | Pair activity on the German Credit model: the accuracy paradox and the two denominators on paper, then counting the four cells of the confusion matrix with masks, precision, recall and F1 by hand, a comparison in which the more accurate model (no `scale_pos_weight`) misses more defaulters and costs the bank more, and reading the classification report's macro and weighted averages. One partner makes small edits to given code; the other records answers in the Canvas quiz. |
| [Threshold_Pair](Threshold_Pair.ipynb) | [18_1_4 ROC, AUC and Threshold Tuning](../18_classification/18_1_Classification_Basics/18_1_4_ROC_AUC_Threshold_Tuning.ipynb) | Pair activity on the 18_1_4 German Credit model: ROC points and AUC as a ranking probability worked by hand, a cost comparison of two thresholds with its break-even cost ratio, then why out-of-fold predictions need `predict_proba`, why `scale_pos_weight` changes recall at 0.5 but barely changes AUC, how the cost-optimal threshold moves with the cost ratio while AUC stays fixed, and why the threshold must not be chosen on the test set. One partner makes small edits to given code; the other records answers in the Canvas quiz. |
| [Fraud_Pair](Fraud_Pair.ipynb) | [18_1_5 Credit Card Fraud](../18_classification/18_1_Classification_Basics/18_1_5_creditcardfraud.ipynb), [18_1_6 Nested CV](../18_classification/18_1_Classification_Basics/18_1_6_ccfraud_with_nested_crosseval.ipynb) | Pair activity on the 18_1_5 credit card fraud model: accuracy, FPR and precision when fraud is rare, worked by hand, and the break-even cost ratio between two thresholds; then what the ROC curve hides between 80% and 90% recall, what F-beta asks you to decide, why Youden's J is the cost-optimal threshold for a cost ratio equal to the class ratio (about 578 to 1), and why ranking models by cost at a fixed threshold (18_1_6's pitfall) picks the wrong model. One partner makes small edits to given code; the other records answers in the Canvas quiz. |
| [Telco_Pair](Telco_Pair.ipynb) | [18_1_9_3 Telco Churn](../18_classification/18_1_Classification_Basics/18_1_9_3_Telco_Churn.ipynb) | Capstone pair activity for 18_1 on the Telco churn model (cleaning, preprocessing and the model are given): the costs of calling no one or everyone and the break-even probability, worked by hand; then the model against those strategies, why the ROC curve is more informative when churn is common than when fraud is rare, the default, Youden's J and cost-optimal thresholds (J lands above the cost-optimal threshold here because the class ratio is below the cost ratio), and what a limit on the number of calls does to the threshold and the cost. One partner makes small edits to given code; the other records answers in the Canvas quiz. |
| [LogReg_Titanic_Pair](LogReg_Titanic_Pair.ipynb) | [18_2_0_2 Plain Language](../18_classification/18_2_LogisticRegression/18_2_0_2_LogReg_Plain_Language.md) and [18_2_0_3 Math](../18_classification/18_2_LogisticRegression/18_2_0_3_LogReg_Math.md) (which replace 18_2_0_1), [18_2_1 Titanic](../18_classification/18_2_LogisticRegression/18_2_1_LogReg_Intro_w_Titanic.ipynb) | Pair activity on the 18_2_1 Titanic logistic regression model. On paper, with a worked example for each question: converting among probability, odds and log-odds, calculating one passenger's predicted probability, and using an odds ratio. In the notebook: reading coefficients as odds ratios, seeing why the same odds ratio for sex raises different passengers' probabilities by different amounts, and explaining that with the sigmoid curve. Extra credit covers calibration and AUC. One partner makes small edits to given code; the other records answers in the Canvas quiz. |
| [LogReg_Interpret_Pair](LogReg_Interpret_Pair.ipynb) | [18_2_2 Interpretability](../18_classification/18_2_LogisticRegression/18_2_2_LogReg_Interpretability.ipynb) | Pair activity on the 18_2_2 Titanic models: a confidence interval for an odds ratio worked by hand and odds ratios written as plain sentences, then the forest plot next to XGBoost's feature importance (which features have intervals that include 1, and why `fare_log` climbs the ranking under total gain), one passenger's prediction split into feature contributions with two codings of sex, and why measuring contributions from the average passenger gives the same reasons under both codings. One partner makes small edits to given code; the other records answers in the Canvas quiz. |
| [Neuron_Network_Pair](Neuron_Network_Pair.ipynb) | [18_2_3 LogReg to Neural Networks](../18_classification/18_2_LogisticRegression/18_2_3_LogReg_to_NeuralNetworks.ipynb) | Pair activity for 18_2_3 on the Titanic and XOR data: one neuron's output computed by hand and checked against `predict_proba` (and what a single weight on `sex_f` assumes about every class), why no straight line solves XOR and why a hidden layer needs a nonlinear activation to solve it (10 random seeds per activation), then the 18_2_3 MLP against the logistic regression in accuracy and in the odds ratio for `sex_f`, which is one number for the logistic regression and different for every passenger in the MLP. One partner makes small edits to given code; the other records answers in the Canvas quiz. |
